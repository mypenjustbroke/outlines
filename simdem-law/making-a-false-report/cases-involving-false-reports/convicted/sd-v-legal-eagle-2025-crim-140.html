Type: Case
Topic: False Report
Judge: ppatpat
Date: November 7, 2025
Result: Convicted

SD v Legal Eagle 2025 Crim 140

  • Link
  • Facts: ∆ was charged of 1 count of Making A False Report and 3 counts of Making a False Report Hoax for pinging Hope's Hand 4 times.
    • Hope's Hand is a statutory legal authority under the HOPE Act.
    • π's theory for Art. 22a relied on the assumption that Hope's Hand was a statutory legal authority, that a role ping alerts all holders at once and therefore constitutes a mass summon “in any way” under Article 22a §1.2; and that “ling”-only pings are manifestly without legal merit.
    • ∆ conceded the 4 pings and offered no evidence.
      • Instead, ∆ argued 3 things:
      1. Hope's Hand is not a legal authority, rather a medical/volunteer service outside the legal system.
      2. A ping is not a filing, and a single ping to a small role is not a mass summon.
      3. Legal merit cannot be assessed for what it called a medical alert.
  • Issues:
    1. Does Hope’s Hand fall within “other legal authorities” in Article 22a?
    2. Is a role ping a “filing of a report” under Article 22a §1.2?
    3. Are the pings manifestly without legal merit under Article 22a?
  • Holdings:
    1. Yes. Hope’s Hand is an “other legal authority” under Article 22a.
    2. Hope’s Hand was a statutory authority with a public, role-based intake for crisis alerts, so the defendant’s four pings therefore constituted filings of reports under §1.1.
    3. No one, regardless of how low their experience or expertise, could reasonably believe there was legal merit to summoning a legal authority on that basis.
  • Reasoning:
    1. On first issue:
      1. Relied on the canon of construction in In re Article 30 of the Civil Code 2025, 2025 SDCR 2.
        1. “Statutes are to be read such that no word is treated as meaningless…”, and “In a system of law, words are everything. If they are not treated with care, then law becomes only will, not reason.” Id. at (30)–(30.3).
        2. To this, reading “other legal authorities” to mean only SDBI would erase “other.”
        3. The statute names SDBI and a broader class.
      2. A body like Hope’s Hand, constituted by the HOPE Act with delegated intervention powers, therefore fits the text; to hold otherwise would “treat statutory language as surplusage."
    2. On second issue:
      1. File a report” in §1.1 was read as:
        1. delivering the substance of a report through the authority’s established procedure for receiving reports.
      2. Hope’s Hand is found to be a statutory authority with a public, role-based intake for crisis alerts:
        1. Thus, the defendant’s four pings therefore constituted filings of reports under §1.1.
    3. On third issue:
      1. Article 22a is not concerned with clinical or moral merit; it uses the legal phrase “manifestly without legal merit” and requires the Court to consider whether the person knew or ought to know that.
        1. The question is thus whether the ping provides any law-relevant basis to invoke a legal authority’s powers.
      2. Content consisting of nothing but “ling” provides no facts, no description, and no threshold-qualifying circumstance.
        1. That is manifestly meritless in the legal sense.
  • Judgment: Convicted and sentenced to a 64 hour mute, at 16 hours per conviction.
